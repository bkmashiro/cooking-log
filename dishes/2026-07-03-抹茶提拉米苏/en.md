## Method

### Make the matcha soaking liquid

Sift the matcha. Use **6–8g matcha powder** and whisk it with a little 70–80°C hot water into a smooth paste. Add the remaining hot water, milk, and 10–15g sugar, then mix well. Let it cool before assembling.

Avoid boiling water directly on the matcha; it can make it bitter and clumpy.

### Make the cream

Let the mascarpone soften slightly at room temperature, then stir just until smooth. Whip the cream with 40g sugar to **soft-medium peaks**: it should hold visible trails but still look soft and slowly flowing.

Fold the whipped cream into the mascarpone in 2–3 additions. Stop once the mixture is smooth and thick; do not over-beat.

### Assemble

This makes **4 transparent cups of 180–220ml** or one small **15×15cm dish**.

Break the ladyfingers to fit the container. Dip them very quickly in the matcha liquid, about **1 second per side**. Do not soak. Layer in this order: ladyfingers, cream, ladyfingers, cream. Smooth the top.

Cover and refrigerate for at least **6 hours**, preferably overnight.

### Garnish

Right before serving, dust the top with the remaining **2–3g matcha powder**. Add strawberries, blueberries, white chocolate shavings, and birthday candles if serving as a birthday dessert.

---

## Key Tips

- Use dry, firm Savoiardi ladyfingers rather than soft sponge-cake fingers.
- Matcha milk softens biscuits faster than coffee, so dip briefly and lift immediately.
- Do not whip the cream too stiff; very stiff cream is harder to fold smoothly into mascarpone.
- Do not beat mascarpone aggressively, as it can split.
- Dust matcha only before serving; if refrigerated after dusting, it absorbs moisture, darkens, and can taste more bitter.

## Feedback

A birthday dessert option designed to be easier and more reliable than chiffon cake: no oven, stable assembly, and a clean Japanese-style dessert-shop look. A 30g bag of matcha is more than enough; this 4-serving batch uses about 10g total.
