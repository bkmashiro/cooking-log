## Method

### Prepare the pork and century eggs

Slice the lean pork into thin strips or slices. Mix with light soy sauce, white pepper, starch, and oil, then marinate for **10 minutes**. If using pork mince, simply season and mix.

Dice the century eggs and divide them into two portions: one goes in early to flavour the congee base, and the other goes in at the end for texture.

### Make the quick congee

Put the cooked rice and water into a large pot. Bring to a boil, then break up the rice with a spoon. Reduce to medium-low and simmer for **15–20 minutes**, stirring occasionally, until the grains bloom and the congee thickens.

Add the ginger and half of the century egg. Simmer for another **5 minutes** so the flavour melts into the base.

### Add the pork and season

Keep the congee at a gentle simmer. Add the marinated pork and stir quickly to separate the pieces. Cook for **1–2 minutes**, just until the pork is cooked through.

Add the remaining century egg, then season with salt and white pepper. Finish with spring onion and a drizzle of sesame oil.

---

## Key Tips

- Cooked rice is the fastest version; for raw rice, wash the rice, mix with a little oil and salt for 10 minutes, then cook with about 10 times its volume of water for 40–60 minutes
- Add the pork at the end and cook just until done, or it will turn tough
- Add century egg in two stages: one part flavours the base, the other keeps its texture
- Be generous with white pepper; ginger and white pepper are what make this congee aromatic
- A large pot helps prevent boiling over, but stir the bottom occasionally once the congee thickens
